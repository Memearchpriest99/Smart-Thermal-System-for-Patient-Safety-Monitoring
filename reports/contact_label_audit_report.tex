\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Contact detection had plateaued at a pooled cross-validated $F_1\approx44\%$, previously
attributed entirely to data scarcity. This report documents a systematic label-quality
audit which found that \textbf{roughly half of all scoring errors were annotation
problems, not detection failures}: two videos were never annotated for touch and one was
block-labelled. On the correctly-annotated subset the champion rule-based method
(config-D) reaches \textbf{$F_1=76.9\%$} with 97.8\% recall (with a $\pm2$-frame boundary
tolerance), and the pixel-based Thermo-X3D reaches \textbf{$F_1=78.5\%$} at a 1.5\% false-alarm
rate. Retraining the learned models on clean labels only \emph{reduced} their performance
--- demonstrating that at the current dataset size, label \emph{quantity} outweighs label
\emph{purity}. The audit also uncovered and fixed a checkpoint-persistence bug that made
every saved MV-STGCN model unusable, and the properly-measured MV-STGCN is shown to be
structurally unsuited to the task. The single highest-value next action is re-annotating
the three defective videos.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background and Motivation}
% ============================================================

The previous investigation cycle (see \emph{Touch Detection --- Detailed Investigation
Report}) converged on \textbf{config-D} as the best contact detector: MobileNet-SSD person
detection on \emph{raw} frames, blob segmentation on the \emph{Tateno residual}, a
two-body-merge rule (fire only when exactly two people share one warm connected
component), and $T_1$ temporal morphology (opening $\ell_o=2$, closing $\ell_c=7$).
Its honest 4-fold cross-validated score was a pooled $F_1=44.3\%$ with a fold standard
deviation of $\pm24$\,pt, and the conclusion at the time was that the effort was
\emph{data-limited}: further algorithmic tuning would chase noise.

That conclusion prompted the question this report answers: \emph{are we actually
detecting things wrong, or is the data mislabelled?} A frame-level diagnostic of config-D
over the entire labelled dataset (2{,}753 annotated frames across 17 sessions) gave the
starting confusion matrix:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccccccc}
\toprule
 & TP & FN & FP & TN & Precision & Recall & $F_1$ & FAR \\
\midrule
config-D, all data & 112 & 76 & 222 & 2343 & 33.5\% & 59.6\% & 42.9\% & 8.7\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The error mass is heavily concentrated: three videos account for
$\sim$50\% of all errors, and all three turn out to have defective labels.

% ============================================================
\section{Label-Quality Audit}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Method}

Three independent probes were combined
(script: \texttt{scripts/\_analyze\_label\_quality.py}):

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Error-position analysis.} For every false positive and false negative,
    the distance (in annotated-frame steps) to the nearest frame carrying the
    \emph{opposite} label. Errors hugging a label transition indicate fuzzy boundary
    annotation; errors deep inside a uniformly-labelled span indicate either systematic
    mislabels or genuine detection failure.
    \item \textbf{Visual audit.} Three-camera thermal strips of every suspicious segment
    were rendered with SSD boxes overlaid
    (\texttt{scripts/\_render\_suspect\_frames.py}, output in
    \texttt{outputs/label\_audit/}) and inspected frame by frame.
    \item \textbf{Source cross-check.} The per-frame \texttt{touch} column in
    \texttt{labels.xlsx} was compared against the exported
    \texttt{contact\_labels.csv}, and the workbook's review status (green tab
    convention) was checked for every session.
\end{enumerate}

The CSV mirrors the workbook exactly, so all defects originate in the annotation
itself. Notably, \textbf{no sheet tab in \texttt{labels.xlsx} is marked green} --- by the
project's own convention, no session's touch labels have ever been reviewed.

\subsection{Findings: three defective videos}

    never annotated \\
\texttt{3ppl\_dance} & 0 / 399 & 25 FP & 11\% of all FP &
    never annotated \\
\texttt{3pp\_surprise} & 198 / 207 (96\%) & 48 FN & 63\% of all FN &

\bottomrule

\caption{The three suspect videos. ``\texttt{touch}=1 rows'' counts positive rows in

\end{table}

\textbf{\texttt{the\_more\_the\_merrier}} (350 annotated frames): every row carries
\texttt{touch}=0, yet the renders show three to four people clustered with overlapping
bounding boxes for long stretches (frames 117--280). Five consecutive-error runs of up to
26 frames coincide with visibly adjacent/overlapping bodies. A video with this name and
literally zero touch marks is a default-value artefact, not an annotation decision.

\textbf{\texttt{3ppl\_dance}} (133 frames): the same all-zero pattern, with dancers
visibly adjacent or overlapping during the flagged runs (frames 8--11, 94--101).

\textbf{\texttt{3pp\_surprise}} (69 frames): 96\% of frames are labelled positive,
\emph{including frame~0}, where the participants are clearly separated in two of the
three cameras. 43 of its 48 false negatives lie more than 5 annotated frames from any
negatively-labelled frame --- the signature of a whole-clip (``block'') label rather than
frame-by-frame annotation.

\subsection{Boundary fuzziness}

Of the 298 total errors, 66 (39 FP + 27 FN) sit within $\pm2$ annotated frames of a
label transition. These reflect ordinary human imprecision about exactly when contact
begins and ends, and motivate reporting metrics both exactly and with a $\pm2$-frame
tolerance.

\subsection{Genuine failure modes}

The audit also \emph{confirmed} two real detection failures, which no relabelling will fix:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Hug blob-merge} (\texttt{2ppl\_hug}, frames 60--72): the two embracing
    people merge into a \emph{single} SSD detection in \emph{all three} cameras
    ($\sim$1.1 boxes/camera). Any detector that counts bodies before measuring distance
    is structurally blind here. This is the hard case the pixel-based Thermo-X3D was
    designed for (\S4.4.3.3 of the engineering report).
    \item \textbf{Proximity vs.\ touch} (\texttt{2ppl\_fight} 58--82,
    \texttt{3pplhedroncolider} mid-segments): people standing very close without
    touching. At $80\times62$ thermal resolution this discrimination is genuinely hard,
    though some of these labels are judgment calls worth a second look during
    re-annotation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Clean-Label Benchmark of All Detector Families}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Protocol}

Every algorithm family investigated to date was re-scored on the \textbf{clean subset}:
the 14 correctly-annotated videos (2{,}201 annotated frames, 122 positive), pooled over
all their labelled frames (script: \texttt{scripts/\_eval\_clean\_labels.py}; results:
\texttt{reports/clean\_label\_benchmark.json}). Existing checkpoints and previously-tuned
parameters were used --- no retuning on the clean subset.

\emph{Caveats.} (i)~The SSD checkpoints saw 60--80\% of these frames during training, so
this is a whole-dataset diagnostic applied identically to all methods, not a held-out
benchmark. (ii)~The learned models' checkpoints were trained while the polluted labels
were still in their training split (addressed in \S\ref{sec:retrain}).

\subsection{Discussion}

\textbf{Thermo-X3D was the biggest victim of the bad labels.} Its apparent $F_1$ rises
from $\sim$42\% to 78.5\% --- statistically tied with config-D but with \emph{half} the
false-alarm rate. Crucially, it is the only method that handles \texttt{2ppl\_hug}
(54.5\%), because pixel-based classification survives the blob-merge. This is the
\S4.4.3.3 design thesis, now visible in the numbers.

\textbf{Config-D remains the recall champion} (97.8\% at $\pm2$) and the best at exact
frame alignment; it wins \texttt{2ppl\_fight} decisively.

\textbf{The geometric detector collapses} ($F_1$ 7.7\%). Its previous 27\% was partly
lucky agreement with mislabelled frames. Its signature failure --- 31 false positives on
\texttt{3pp\_standing\_still} --- shows the core flaw: projected floor proximity is not
touch.

\textbf{The false-positive characters are complementary:} Thermo-X3D's only
negative-scene FPs are on \texttt{heater\_in\_middle} (a hot static object); config-D's
are on cigarette scenes (person plus a small hot object). A conjunction of the two is a
plausible precision play, but tuning it now would repeat the noise-chasing the
cross-validation study warned against.

\section{The MV-STGCN Checkpoint-Persistence Bug}
\label{sec:bug}
% ============================================================

During the benchmark, MV-STGCN produced \emph{zero} predictions --- a flat confidence of
$\approx0.03$ on every frame, including obvious contact in \texttt{2ppl\_fight}, although
the same model had shown $\mathrm{FAR}=30\%$ when evaluated in memory directly after
training.

\textbf{Root cause.} \texttt{ContactDetector.\_\_init\_\_} intercepts the
\texttt{homography} argument and stores it on \texttt{self.\_homography},
\emph{outside} the \texttt{\_params} dict that the base-class \texttt{save()} persists.
\texttt{MVSTGCNDetector.\_state\_dict()} saved only the network weights. Consequently
every saved checkpoint reloaded with \texttt{homography=None}; the fusion front-end then
produced zero actors on every frame, the graph input was empty, and the network emitted a
constant confidence. \emph{The weights were always correct --- the front-end was dead.}

\textbf{Why the tests missed it.} The existing round-trip tests called
\texttt{predict} with empty detections (\texttt{([],[],[])}) and no homography ---
so a dead front-end was indistinguishable from the expected behaviour.

\textbf{Fix.} \texttt{\_state\_dict()} now persists the homography alongside the weights
and \texttt{\_load\_state\_dict()} restores it
(\texttt{thermal\_algorithms/}\allowbreak\texttt{contact\_detection/}\allowbreak\texttt{mv\_stgcn.py}); a regression test
(\texttt{test\_homography\_survives\_save\_load}) locks the behaviour in. Both existing
checkpoints were repaired in place by re-injecting the homography and re-saving --- no
retraining required. The repaired checkpoint reproduces the in-memory evaluation exactly.
\texttt{GeometricContactDetector} already persisted its homography correctly; Thermo-X3D
does not use one.

\section{Retraining the Learned Models on Clean Labels}
\label{sec:retrain}
% ============================================================

Both learned detectors were retrained from scratch with the three defective videos
excluded from \emph{both} training and evaluation, under the identical protocol
(per-session timeline split 60/15/25, class-weighted cross-entropy, threshold swept on
validation; scripts: \texttt{scripts/\_retrain\_dl\_clean.py},
\texttt{scripts/\_rescore\_stgcn\_clean.py}; results:
\texttt{reports/dl\_clean\_retrain\_results.json}). The clean training split contains
only \textbf{64 positive windows} (down from $\sim$105 when the suspect videos were
included).

\textbf{Clean retraining made Thermo-X3D \emph{worse}} (78.5\% $\rightarrow$ 62.6\%
pooled $\pm2$). The explanation is data arithmetic: excluding the suspect videos removed
roughly 40\% of the positive training windows. Even \texttt{3pp\_surprise}'s sloppy
block-labels were net-useful --- many of those frames genuinely contain contact, and a
data-starved model benefits more from noisy positives than from their absence. At the
current dataset size, \textbf{label quantity outweighs label purity for the learned
models}. This is the strongest evidence yet for the data-limited conclusion, and it means
re-annotation pays twice: cleaner evaluation \emph{and} more training positives.

\section{Why MV-STGCN Is Structurally Unsuited}
\label{sec:stgcn}
% ============================================================

With the persistence bug fixed, MV-STGCN can finally be measured properly --- and it is
the worst method by a wide margin ($\mathrm{FAR}=25.5\%$ pooled; false positives on
\texttt{2ppl\_walk}, \texttt{1ppl\_single\_forever}, even 60 on \texttt{1person\,cig}'s
test region). This is not a training accident; it follows from the architecture's
position in the pipeline:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{It inherits the geometric detector's input representation.} Its graph
    nodes are actor positions produced by SSD $\rightarrow$ foot-point $\rightarrow$
    homography projection $\rightarrow$ fusion --- exactly the representation that gave
    the geometric detector $F_1=7.7\%$. The self-calibrated homography is accurate to
    roughly $\pm15$\,px in the reference plane, while the contact decision itself lives
    at $\delta\approx18$\,px: \emph{the measurement noise is nearly as large as the
    quantity being measured.} No downstream network can recover information the
    front-end has already destroyed.
    \item \textbf{The blob-merge deletes the evidence exactly when contact happens.} At
    the moment two people touch, SSD returns \emph{one} box; the two graph nodes
    collapse into one. The model therefore never observes ``two nodes at distance
    $\approx0$'' for real contact --- the positive class's defining geometry is
    unrepresentable in its input. What remains learnable is only the \emph{approach}
    dynamics that precede a merge, i.e.\ proximity, not touch.
    \item \textbf{Proximity is what it learns, so proximity is what it fires on.} Every
    close pass (\texttt{2ppl\_walk}), spurious second detection
    (\texttt{1person\,cig}), or tight standing group looks like the pre-contact pattern
    it was trained to associate with the positive class --- hence the 25\% false-alarm
    rate, an order of magnitude above the $\sim$2\% a psychiatric-ward alarm system
    could tolerate.
    \item \textbf{The data budget cannot rescue it.} Discriminating approach-then-touch
    from approach-then-stop using noisy trajectories would need orders of magnitude more
    positive windows than the 64--105 available; the class-weighted loss meanwhile pushes
    the recall-FAR trade-off toward over-firing.
\end{enumerate}

Thermo-X3D avoids all four problems by construction: it reads pixels directly, has no
homography or detection front-end, and the blob-merge that blinds MV-STGCN is its
\emph{signal}. The two learned designs bracket the lesson: \textbf{what decides success
here is not model capacity but which representation survives the moment of contact.}

\textbf{Recommendation:} drop MV-STGCN from the candidate set. Keep the persistence fix
(it is a correctness fix for the shared checkpoint format), keep the architecture
description in the engineering report as a negative result with the analysis above.

\section{Conclusions and Recommended Actions}
% ============================================================

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{The detector was never as bad as the numbers said.} About half the
    measured errors were annotation defects. On correctly-annotated data, config-D
    scores $F_1=76.9\%$ / recall 97.8\% ($\pm2$ tol) and Thermo-X3D $F_1=78.5\%$ at
    FAR 1.5\%.
    \item \textbf{Re-annotate the three defective videos} ($\sim$550 frames:
    \texttt{the\_more\_the\_merrier}, \texttt{3ppl\_dance}, \texttt{3pp\_surprise}).
    This is the highest-leverage action available: it simultaneously enlarges the clean
    evaluation set, adds the dataset's richest genuine-contact footage as training
    positives (the retraining experiment shows the learned models are starving for
    exactly these), and removes the largest source of metric distortion.
    \item \textbf{Lock the method pair:} config-D as the operating detector (recall
    priority), Thermo-X3D (the original checkpoint, pending retraining after
    re-annotation) as the precision-oriented complement and the only answer to the hug
    blob-merge. Revisit a config-D~$\wedge$~X3D conjunction only after re-annotation.
    \item \textbf{Drop MV-STGCN and the geometric detector} from the candidate set for
    the contact scenario (\S\ref{sec:stgcn}); retain them in the engineering report as
    analysed negative results.
    \item \textbf{Institute label review.} No session's touch labels were ever reviewed
    (no green tabs). Frame-exact contact boundaries are worth a defined convention
    during re-annotation (e.g.\ ``first frame of visible limb overlap''), since $\pm2$
    frames of boundary fuzz already accounts for 22\% of residual errors.
    \item \textbf{More recordings are still needed.} The clean subset has only three
    contact scenes and 122 positive frames; the earlier bootstrap analysis ($\sim$900
    positives, 12--16 distinct contact scenes for a reliable 65\%+ estimate) still
    stands.
\end{enumerate}
